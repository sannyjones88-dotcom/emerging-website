FOUNDATION CONCEPT

Understanding Emerging Technologies & IT Trends

Before diving into individual tools, let us understand what makes a technology "emerging" and why it matters to students and society.

What Is an Emerging Technology?

An emerging technology is a radical innovation currently in development or rapidly growing in practical adoption. It has the power to dramatically reshape industries, create new careers, and solve challenges that older systems cannot touch.

Established: Smartphones, 4G, Laptops Emerging: GenAI, Quantum, Brain-Computer Interfaces

What Is Digital Transformation?

Digital transformation is how schools, hospitals, and companies integrate modern digital tools into every area of their operation — changing how work gets done and delivering greater value to people.

"Example: A college replacing paper registration with an instant online student portal and automated advisory system."

How Innovations Move to Mainstream

Technologies travel through a journey: Lab Research → Prototype → Early Adopters → Mass Adoption → Everyday Standard. Understanding this curve helps you prepare for the jobs and skills of tomorrow.

85% of jobs that today's students will fill in 2035 will involve tools that are still emerging today.

TOPIC 14

4-Step Framework for Choosing Tech

Never adopt technology just because it's popular or shiny. Always match the tool to the genuine human problem.

1
Identify the Real Problem First:

Don't buy AI just to have AI. Ask: "Is the problem slow grading? Lost records? Low student engagement?"

2
Evaluate Total Readiness & Budget:

Can the school afford maintenance, high-speed Wi-Fi, and software licenses year after year?

3
Train the People (Human Centricity):

A high-tech smart board is useless if teachers never receive friendly, stress-free training on how to use it.

4
Pilot Test & Measure Outcomes:

Roll out to 2 classrooms first. Measure if grades or enthusiasm improved before spending on the whole campus.

Real College Scenario: St. Jude Tech Academy

Faced with high dropout rates in first-year programming, the school considered buying 100 expensive VR headsets ($50,000). Applying this framework, they realized VR wasn't the issue — students needed immediate 24/7 homework feedback. They chose an affordable AI coding tutor ($3,000) instead. Result: Homework completion rose by 42% at a fraction of the cost.

TOPIC 15

Future Implications for IT & Society

Balancing boundless innovation with ethics, privacy, human dignity, and sustainable employment.

The Future of Jobs & Careers

Repetitive routine tasks are automated, while roles emphasizing creative problem solving, empathy, systems design, and AI orchestration become highly valued.

Privacy & Data Sovereignty

As IoT sensors and facial recognition expand, strict legal guardrails (like GDPR and Philippine Data Privacy Act) are vital to ensure human dignity.

The Digital Divide & Equity

If advanced AI and satellite internet are only available in wealthy urban areas, rural students fall behind. Responsible technology must be inclusive and accessible.

Green Computing & Energy Needs

Data centers training large AI models and validating blockchains consume gigawatts of electricity. The future demands carbon-neutral server infrastructure.
